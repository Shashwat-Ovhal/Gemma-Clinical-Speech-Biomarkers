## Where you stand right now
With the full mPower loop closed (real audio → real surveys → Gemma synthesis), you have something genuinely publishable — but only if you add evaluation rigor. The system working is not the same as the system being scientifically validated. Right now you have a functioning pipeline with no statistical evidence that it works better than simpler alternatives.

## Level 1 → what you need to actually submit
The minimum viable paper requires three things added to what you have. First, run stratified 5-fold cross-validation on your mPower cohort — you need at least 100 patients for this to be meaningful, and the fold splits must be stratified by PD/HC label. Second, implement one traditional baseline: SVM trained on the same Jitter/Shimmer/HNR/F0_Std features you already extract. This takes maybe 20 lines of sklearn. Report AUC-ROC with 95% CI for both. Third, include 3 concrete Gemma output examples in the paper showing the clinical note for a stable patient, a deteriorating patient, and an ambiguous case. That's qualitative but reviewers at this tier will accept it.
This gets you into MDPI Diagnostics or Applied Sciences. Reasonable outcome, genuinely indexed, and a real publication on your CV.

## Level 2 → the paper worth putting serious effort toward
The jump from Level 1 to Level 2 requires two things that are harder but not out of reach. The most important is the multi-modal fusion metric: you need to formally define how your system combines acoustic evidence (Agent 1) with longitudinal evidence (Agent 2). Right now Gemma just receives both as a prompt string — that's an engineering choice, not a scientific contribution. Define a weighted fusion score (call it the Clinical Synthesis Index or whatever you want), give it a formula, and show that using both inputs outperforms either alone. The second hard thing is the wav2vec 2.0 baseline — this is the current state-of-the-art for speech-based PD detection and reviewers at CBAM will ask why you didn't compare against it. Fine-tuning wav2vec on your mPower audio is achievable in Colab with ~4 hours of compute.
Add SHAP values on your Random Forest features (already in sklearn, 10 lines) and show the longitudinal trend for at least 10 patients over ≥3 timepoints, and you have a Level 2 paper.

## Level 3 → the honest ceiling for this project
IEEE JBHI and npj Digital Medicine require one thing you cannot fake: clinical validation. You need actual clinicians to evaluate Gemma's outputs. Even if it's just 3 medical professionals scoring 20 outputs each on a simple rubric (coherence, factual accuracy, hallucination presence, clinical usefulness — 1 to 5 scale), and you report inter-rater agreement (Cohen's κ), that transforms the paper from "we built a tool" to "we validated a tool." This is the single hardest step because it requires human collaborators outside your lab.
The other Level 3 requirement — the ablation study — is purely computational. Run the pipeline with Agent 2 disabled (audio only), then Agent 1 disabled (history only), then both enabled. The table showing that the combined agentic loop outperforms either agent alone is the empirical proof that the architecture is the contribution.
